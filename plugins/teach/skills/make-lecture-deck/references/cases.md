# Choosing case studies

*"All of these use cases not so interesting...... focus more interesting use cases"*

## What makes a case interesting
- An **AI agent that acts** in a core business process, not a chatbot or a writing helper.
- A **clear before/after number** that a manager cares about: money recovered, time to settle, error rate, cost.
- **Recent** (prefer the last 12 months), from a primary or reputable source.
- An **honest limit**: what stays human, what didn't improve.

## Strong examples by sector (re-check numbers before reuse)
| Sector | Case | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Banking | Banco BV (Brazil): AI agents negotiate overdue loans by voice and WhatsApp | 48% vs 44% recovery, 85% of short-term collections, −67% cost |
| Banking | DBS (Singapore): AI "nudges" in the normal banking app | Shows *how* AI makes money; counted with a control group |
| Insurance | Allianz Project Nemo (Australia): 7 agents settle storm claims, a person approves | −80% time; built in <100 days |
| Supply chain | Walmart + Pactum: AI negotiates with small suppliers | 64% deals vs 20% target; suppliers preferred the AI |
| Travel | Trip.com TripGenie: plans and books the whole trip | ~60% of chats about booking; +400% AI orders |
| Healthcare | Penda Health (Kenya) + OpenAI: AI second opinion on diagnosis and treatment | −16% diagnostic, −13% treatment errors; outcomes unchanged |

Ideas he asked for that are worth searching each time: insurance claims or sales agents; banking credit, sales and debt collection; agents that plan and book a whole trip; clinical decision support.

## Slide template
Pill: `Sector · Company, Country` → title stating what the agent does → three short paragraphs (**The problem. What they did. What changed / The limit.**) with concrete examples → 2–3 big numbers, each with a plain label → sources. Story slides live under a "Successful stories" intro table.

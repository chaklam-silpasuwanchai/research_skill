# Teach Me

Use when `clarify` wasn't enough and the researcher needs to be **guided**, not just told. Optional focus: `/teach-me <topic>` (a concept like power analysis, a tool like git or LaTeX, or a step like running the pilot). No focus → teach what the last answer assumed they knew.

## 1. Find the starting point (one or two questions)
"What do you already know about <topic>?" and "What do you need it for right now?" Start one step below what they know. Use their project as the running example.

## 2. Teach in small steps (3-7 steps)
For each step:
1. **One idea** in two or three plain sentences.
2. **An example** from their project (their data, their conditions, their paper).
3. **Their turn:** a tiny check they can answer or do ("Which of your measures is ordinal?", "Run `git status` and tell me what you see").
4. **Wait.** Right → one line of praise, next step. Not quite → explain the same idea another way (a picture in words, an analogy, a smaller example) and check again. Never move on while they are lost.

Show where they are: "Step 2 of 5".

## 3. Do it together
End with them doing the real thing once on their own project, with you watching and helping (writing the actual power analysis, making the actual commit). Hands-on is the goal; they should be able to do it again without you.

## 4. Wrap up
- A five-line **cheat note** they can keep: the steps, one example, one common mistake.
- Offer to save it in `components/notes/` (or anywhere they like) and to log what they learned in `log.md`.
- Suggest the skill that uses what they just learned (for example `plan-method` after power analysis).

## Rules
Patient and kind; no jargon unless you just taught it; their words from `terms.md`; their explanation language from `AGENTS.md`. Never make them feel slow.

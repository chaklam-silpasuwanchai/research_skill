# Setup Writing Style

Makes `write` sound like the researcher, not like an AI. Optional focus: a file path, link, or "from my drafts".

## 1. Get samples
Ask once: "Do you have past papers of your own (paths or links)? If not, paste 2-3 paragraphs you wrote, or I can learn from your drafts later." Past papers stay **outside** the repo; only the notes are saved. Co-authored papers: ask which sections they wrote.

## 2. Read and note (about 25 lines in `components/voice.md`)
Fill the template headings with concrete, quotable habits, each with a short example from their text:
- sentence length and rhythm; paragraph habits (how they open and close)
- voice and tense ("we", active or passive, present or past)
- signal words and phrases they like; hedging style ("suggests" vs "shows")
- how they state the gap and the contribution
- **keep** (what makes it theirs) and **change** (only slips they want fixed, e.g. article use)
Use their terms from `terms.md`; never add a style rule they don't show.

## 3. Show and adjust
Show the notes as a short list plus one rewritten sample paragraph in their voice. Saving is not critical: save it, say "Saved to voice.md; tell me anything that doesn't sound like you." Adjust from their answer.

## 4. Refresh later
When they edit drafts heavily, offer once to update `voice.md` from their edits (what they changed tells you their style). Log updates in `log.md`.
